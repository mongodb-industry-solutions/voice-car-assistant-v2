SAMPLE CAR MANUAL - VEHICLE OPERATION GUIDE

========================================
CHAPTER 1: DASHBOARD CONTROLS
========================================

WARNING LIGHTS AND INDICATORS

Check Engine Light
The check engine light (yellow engine symbol) illuminates when the vehicle's onboard diagnostic system detects a problem with the emissions control system. If this light comes on while driving, you should have the vehicle serviced as soon as possible. If the light is flashing, this indicates a more serious problem and you should reduce speed and have the vehicle serviced immediately.

Oil Pressure Warning Light
The oil pressure warning light (red oil can symbol) indicates low engine oil pressure. If this light comes on while driving, stop the vehicle immediately and turn off the engine. Check the oil level and add oil if necessary. Do not continue driving if this light remains on as serious engine damage may occur.

Battery Warning Light
The battery warning light indicates a problem with the charging system. If this light comes on while driving, the battery is not being charged and the vehicle may stop running. Have the charging system checked immediately.

Airbag Warning Light
The airbag warning light indicates a problem with the airbag system. If this light stays on or comes on while driving, the airbag system may not function properly in a collision. Have the system checked by a qualified technician immediately.

CHAPTER 2: ENGINE MAINTENANCE
========================================

ENGINE OIL CHANGE PROCEDURE

When to Change Oil
Engine oil should be changed every 5,000 miles or 6 months, whichever comes first. If you frequently drive in severe conditions (dusty roads, extreme temperatures, frequent short trips), change the oil every 3,000 miles.

How to Change Engine Oil
1. Start the engine and let it run for 2-3 minutes to warm up the oil
2. Turn off the engine and wait 5 minutes
3. Place a drain pan under the oil drain plug
4. Remove the drain plug using a 14mm wrench
5. Allow oil to drain completely (approximately 10 minutes)
6. Replace the drain plug and tighten to 25 ft-lbs
7. Remove the old oil filter using an oil filter wrench
8. Apply a thin coat of new oil to the rubber gasket of the new filter
9. Install the new oil filter by hand, tightening until snug
10. Add 4.5 quarts of 5W-30 synthetic oil through the oil filler cap
11. Start the engine and check for leaks
12. Turn off engine and wait 5 minutes
13. Check oil level with dipstick and add more if needed

Oil Type and Capacity
Use 5W-30 synthetic motor oil meeting API SN or higher specification. Engine oil capacity is 4.5 quarts with filter change.

CHAPTER 3: TIRE MAINTENANCE
========================================

TIRE PRESSURE

Recommended Tire Pressure
Front tires: 35 PSI
Rear tires: 33 PSI
Spare tire: 60 PSI

Check tire pressure at least once a month and before long trips. Check pressure when tires are cold (vehicle has not been driven for at least 3 hours). Underinflated tires wear faster and reduce fuel economy. Overinflated tires provide a harsh ride and are more susceptible to damage.

TIRE ROTATION

Tire rotation should be performed every 7,500 miles to ensure even tire wear. The rotation pattern for this vehicle is front to rear on the same side. Do not cross tires from one side to the other.

TIRE REPLACEMENT

Replace tires when tread depth reaches 2/32 inch (1.6mm). Use the penny test: insert a penny into the tread groove with Lincoln's head upside down. If you can see the top of Lincoln's head, the tire should be replaced.

CHAPTER 4: BRAKE SYSTEM
========================================

BRAKE FLUID

Check brake fluid level monthly. The brake fluid reservoir is located on the driver's side of the engine compartment. Fluid level should be between MIN and MAX marks. If level is low, add DOT 4 brake fluid. Do not overfill.

If brake fluid level drops significantly, have the brake system inspected for leaks immediately. Low brake fluid may indicate worn brake pads or a leak in the system.

BRAKE PAD REPLACEMENT

Brake pads typically last 30,000 to 70,000 miles depending on driving conditions. Warning signs of worn brake pads include:
- Squealing or grinding noise when braking
- Brake pedal pulsation
- Increased stopping distance
- Brake warning light on dashboard

Have brake pads inspected annually or whenever you suspect a problem.

CHAPTER 5: BATTERY AND ELECTRICAL
========================================

BATTERY MAINTENANCE

The battery is maintenance-free but should be inspected regularly. Clean battery terminals monthly to prevent corrosion. If terminals appear corroded, clean with a mixture of baking soda and water using a wire brush.

Battery typical lifespan: 3-5 years

Signs of battery failure:
- Engine cranks slowly when starting
- Dim headlights
- Electrical accessories work intermittently
- Battery warning light on dashboard

JUMP STARTING PROCEDURE

If the battery is dead:
1. Position the helper vehicle close enough for jumper cables to reach
2. Turn off both vehicles
3. Connect positive (+) cable to dead battery positive terminal
4. Connect other end of positive cable to helper battery positive terminal
5. Connect negative (-) cable to helper battery negative terminal
6. Connect other end of negative cable to unpainted metal surface on dead vehicle (not battery)
7. Start helper vehicle and let run for 5 minutes
8. Attempt to start dead vehicle
9. Once started, disconnect cables in reverse order
10. Let vehicle run for at least 30 minutes to recharge battery

CHAPTER 6: FUEL SYSTEM
========================================

FUEL REQUIREMENTS

This vehicle requires unleaded gasoline with an octane rating of 87 or higher (regular grade). Using fuel with lower octane may cause engine knocking and reduced performance. Premium fuel (91+ octane) is not required but may improve performance.

Fuel tank capacity: 14.5 gallons

FUEL ECONOMY

Expected fuel economy under normal driving conditions:
City: 28 MPG
Highway: 35 MPG
Combined: 31 MPG

Factors affecting fuel economy:
- Aggressive acceleration and braking
- Excessive idling
- Underinflated tires
- Excess weight in vehicle
- Using air conditioning
- Driving at high speeds

========================================
CHAPTER 7: COOLING SYSTEM
========================================

COOLANT CHECK AND REPLACEMENT

Check coolant level monthly when engine is cold. The coolant reservoir is located on the passenger side of the engine compartment. Level should be between MIN and MAX marks.

Use only 50/50 mixture of ethylene glycol coolant and distilled water. Never add pure coolant or pure water.

Coolant should be replaced every 30,000 miles or 3 years. Old coolant loses its ability to prevent freezing and corrosion.

OVERHEATING

If the temperature gauge shows overheating:
1. Turn off air conditioning
2. Turn on heater to maximum heat and fan speed
3. If still overheating, pull over safely and turn off engine
4. Do not remove radiator cap while engine is hot
5. Wait at least 30 minutes for engine to cool
6. Check coolant level when cool and add if necessary
7. If problem persists, have vehicle towed to service facility

========================================
CHAPTER 8: TRANSMISSION
========================================

AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION FLUID

Check transmission fluid level monthly with engine running and transmission in Park. The dipstick is located near the back of the engine. Fluid should be between MIN and MAX marks and should be bright red in color. Dark or burnt-smelling fluid indicates the need for service.

Transmission fluid should be changed every 60,000 miles under normal conditions, or every 30,000 miles under severe conditions (towing, frequent stop-and-go driving, extreme temperatures).

TRANSMISSION OPERATION

This vehicle is equipped with a 6-speed automatic transmission. Gear positions:
- P (Park): Use when parked. Vehicle cannot be started unless in Park or Neutral
- R (Reverse): Use for backing up
- N (Neutral): Use when being towed or for extended idling
- D (Drive): Normal forward driving for all speeds
- S (Sport): Holds lower gears longer for more responsive performance
- L (Low): For steep hills or towing. Limits transmission to lower gears

Never shift into Park or Reverse while vehicle is moving forward.
